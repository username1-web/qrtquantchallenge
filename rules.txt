
Overview
In this contest you will engage in alpha research to develop systematic strategies for trading stocks on a daily basis using price and volume based features.

By leveraging data analysis, statistical techniques, and machine learning tools, you will be required to identify patterns and build predictive models for profitable trading. The research will involve analyzing over 15 years of historical market data to discover robust factors that consistently generate returns across different market conditions.

The dataset provided contains features derived from daily high, low, close, open and volume of stocks. The identity of stocks is anonymized, and is not relevant to our trading setup.

Your team will have access to features spanning from 2005 to 2025 (both inclusive) to develop portfolio models. Additionally, you will have access to daily stock returns from 2005 to 2019 (both inclusive), which can be used to compute the Sharpe Ratio of your portfolio during this period.

However, daily stock returns from 2020 to 2025 (both inclusive) will not be available, as this period serves as the Testing period. The final evaluation metric will be the Net Sharpe Ratio during this testing period, which measures risk-adjusted portfolio returns net of all trading costs.

Features
Following are the features in features.parquet file derived from open, high, low, close, volume of stocks:

relative_strength_index: It measures the speed and magnitude of recent price changes to assess whether a stock is overbought or oversold. An RSI value above 70 suggests overbought conditions, while a value below 30 indicates oversold conditions, signaling potential reversals.
williams_r: It is a momentum indicator that measures the level of the closing price relative to the highest high over a given period, typically 14 days. It ranges from -100 to 0, where values below -80 indicate oversold conditions and values above -20 suggest overbought conditions.
volatility_20: It measures the standard deviation of returns over a 20-day period, capturing the magnitude of price fluctuations. Higher values indicate increased market uncertainty and risk, while lower values suggest stability.
volatility_60: It measures the standard deviation of returns over a 60-day period, reflecting longer-term price fluctuations. Higher values indicate greater market uncertainty and potential risk, while lower values suggest more stable price movements.
trend_1_3: It calculates the difference between the short-term exponentially weighted moving average (EWMA) of returns with a span of 1 and the long-term EWMA with a span of 3. This difference is then divided by its exponentially weighted standard deviation with a span of 3, helping to identify the strength and direction of the trend.
trend_5_20: It calculates the difference between the short-term exponentially weighted moving average (EWMA) of returns with a span of 5 and the long-term EWMA with a span of 20. This difference is then divided by its exponentially weighted standard deviation with a span of 20, helping to identify the strength and direction of the trend.
trend_20_60: It calculates the difference between the short-term exponentially weighted moving average (EWMA) of returns with a span of 20 and the long-term EWMA with a span of 60. This difference is then divided by its exponentially weighted standard deviation with a span of 20, helping to identify the strength and direction of the trend.
average_true_range: It measures market volatility by averaging the true range over a specified period, typically 14 days. The true range is the greatest of three values: the current high minus the current low, the absolute difference between the current high and the previous close, or the absolute difference between the current low and the previous close.
macd: It is a trend-following momentum indicator that calculates the difference between the 12-day exponential moving average (EMA) and the 26-day EMA of the closing price. This difference is then smoothed using a 9-day EMA to generate trading signals, where positive values indicate bullish momentum and negative values suggest bearish momentum.
trix: It is a momentum oscillator that calculates the 1-day percentage change of the triple-smoothed exponential moving average (EMA) of the closing price over a 15-day window. Positive TRIX values indicate increasing momentum, while negative values suggest weakening momentum or a potential downtrend.
commodity_channel_index: It measures the deviation of the typical price (average of high, low, and close) from its 20-day simple moving average (SMA), scaled by its mean absolute deviation. Positive values indicate strong upward momentum, while negative values suggest downward momentum, with overbought and oversold levels typically set at +100 and -100.
chande_momentum_oscillator: It measures the difference between the sum of gains and the sum of losses over a 14-day window, normalized to a scale of -100 to 100. Higher values indicate strong upward momentum, while lower values suggest strong downward momentum, with overbought and oversold levels typically set at +50 and -50.
ichimoku: It is a comprehensive trend-following system that calculates the conversion line (9-period midpoint of high and low) and the base line (26-period midpoint of high and low) to determine short- and medium-term trends. The average of these two lines forms the Ichimoku cloud, which helps identify support, resistance, and trend direction.
know_sure_thing: It is a momentum oscillator that calculates a weighted sum of the rate of change (ROC) of the closing price over four different periods (10, 15, 20, and 30 days) smoothed using simple moving averages (10, 10, 10, and 15 days, respectively). Positive values indicate bullish momentum, while negative values suggest bearish momentum.
ultimate_oscillator: It is a momentum indicator that calculates a weighted average of three different periods of the buying pressure (BP) to true range (TR) ratio, typically 7, 14, and 28 days. It aims to reduce false signals by incorporating multiple timeframes, with values above 70 indicating overbought conditions and below 30 suggesting oversold conditions.
aroon: It measures the difference between time since the highest high and lowest low over a 26-day period to determine the strength and direction of a trend.
stochastic_oscillator: It measures the position of the closing price relative to the 14-day high-low range to assess momentum. It ranges from 0 to 100, with values above 80 indicating overbought conditions and below 20 suggesting oversold conditions, often used to identify potential reversals.
on_balance_volume: It tracks cumulative volume flow to measure buying and selling pressure. It adds volume on up days and subtracts volume on down days, with increasing OBV confirming an uptrend and decreasing OBV signaling a downtrend.
ease_of_movement: It measures the relationship between price movement and volume, helping to identify how easily a stock's price moves. A high EMV value indicates that the price is rising with little volume, suggesting strong upward momentum, while a low or negative EMV signals downward pressure.
chaikin_money_flow: It measures money flow volume over a 20-day period to assess buying and selling pressure. Positive values indicate accumulation (buying pressure), while negative values suggest distribution (selling pressure).
accumulation_distribution_index: It measures the cumulative money flow volume to determine the strength of buying or selling pressure. It calculates a Closeness Value (CLV) using the high, low, and close prices, then multiplies it by volume to track whether money is flowing into (accumulation) or out of (distribution) an asset.
volume: The total number of shares traded during the trading day

Note the following before proceeding to write this function:

The get_weights function is designed to calculate stock positions or portfolio weights for the current trading day based on the provided feature data and stock universe constraints.
The function accepts two arguments: features, a pandas.DataFrame where level 0 columns are pandas.DataFrame objects containing historical feature data, and today_universe, a pandas.Series object that identifies the stocks eligible for trading on the current day.
In features, all columnar Dataframes of level 0 share the same shape, chronological datetime index, and identical column ordering corresponding to stock identifiers.
The function is invoked daily, leveraging the feature data clipped up to (but not including) the current trading day to ensure no forward-looking bias.
The today_universe Series has stock identifiers as its index and binary values (1 for inclusion in the tradable universe, 0 otherwise) to constrain the eligible stocks.
The function outputs a dictionary where the keys are strings representing stock identifiers, and the values are floats representing the computed weights/positions for the current trading day.
The dictionary should contain only those stock identifiers as the keys for which the today_universe is 1. We will not evaluate the results if any other stock identifier is in the returned dictionary.
